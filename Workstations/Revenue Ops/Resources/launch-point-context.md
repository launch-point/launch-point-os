## Ideal Client Profile

### Who They Are

Ministry leaders — most commonly pastors, but also staff at churches, parachurch organizations, and other Christian ministry organizations — who are transitioning into non-vocational careers. They are almost always men. They typically have 10–25+ years of ministry leadership experience and are highly competent in their field, but feel lost translating that competence to the marketplace.

### The Three Entry Situations

**1. Currently employed in ministry, ready to leave**
They are still working at a church or ministry but want out. The ministry role requires their beliefs to align with their paycheck in a way that has become constraining, or the culture has become unhealthy enough that they don't want to work for another ministry again. They need a break from full-time vocational ministry.

**2. Unemployed after ministry**
They have left a ministry role — either by choice or by being laid off or fired — and are now actively looking for work. The urgency is higher. They may have been out of work for weeks or months.

**3. One or two jobs removed from ministry, still not right**
They made an initial transition out of ministry into whatever job came first, but it isn't working. They're underpaid, feel aimless, or feel like they've lost the leadership level and vocational identity they had in ministry. They want to recalibrate and find the right fit.

### Why They're Leaving Ministry

Clients typically leave for one or more of these reasons:

- **Theological or cultural conflict:** They have a crisis of conviction — either a theological difference they can no longer reconcile, or a leadership/culture problem so serious it has soured them on ministry work entirely.
- **Family pressure:** The demands of ministry are harming their marriage, their relationship with their kids, or their ability to maintain friendships. There is often an explicit ultimatum involved — from a spouse or from within themselves — that they cannot continue to prioritize ministry over their family.
- **Financial pressure:** Ministry pay has not kept pace with the cost of living. Kids are getting older (college, weddings, cars), debt is accumulating, or a growing family requires more space and resources. They are running out of room financially and know something has to change.

### The Moment They Find Todd

By the time most clients reach out, they have already tried on their own and hit a wall. They have been applying to jobs with very low or zero interview response rates. They are discouraged and beginning to doubt whether the transition is even possible. The typical entry point is one or more of these:

- "I've applied to dozens of jobs and can't get an interview."
- "I don't know what job titles I would even want."
- "I don't know how to talk about my ministry experience in a way that makes sense to a hiring manager."
- "I've tried networking but I don't know how to make it work."

### The Core Fear

Underneath the tactical frustration is a deeper fear: that they will have to give up the leadership, the impact, and the vocational identity they spent 20+ years building, and end up in a job they hate — one that also doesn't pay enough to support their family. The shorthand for this fear that clients use is something like: "I guess I'll just have to go work at Costco or Trader Joe's."

They are afraid of diminishment — of going backward vocationally after spending decades leading, building, and living in their gifts.

### What They Want

The ideal outcome is not just a job. It is a new identity: a person who is still extremely competent and successful professionally, but is no longer sacrificing their family's stability, freedom, and financial wellbeing for a calling. In practical terms, the win looks like:

- A job title and role that reflects their leadership level
- Salary that actually covers the cost of living — and then some
- The ability to pay for vacations, date nights, home repairs
- Saving for the first time, or more aggressively
- Long-term financial stability for their kids
- No longer choosing between ministry and family

### How They See Their Own Experience

Most clients know they are good at ministry. They do not know if the marketplace has a place for that. They tend to significantly undervalue themselves financially — on sales calls, when asked what salary they want, they often default to a lower number than what they actually hope for. They need help understanding what is possible and what they are actually worth in the marketplace.

## The Lead-to-Client Journey

### Where Leads Come From

Approximately 70% of clients are referrals — either direct introductions from former clients, alumni, or referral partners, or warm recommendations where multiple people told someone to reach out to Todd. These leads often come in via:

- Group text to Todd's work phone number
- Email thread
- LinkedIn DM

The remaining leads come from:

- **Email nurture list (~1,500 subscribers):** Three emails per week — Monday (video testimonial), Wednesday (live workshop replay), Friday (strategic newsletter). Workshops are promoted to the list as well.
- **Quiz funnel:** Built in Score App, feeds into Kit for email nurture. This is already fully automated.
- **LinkedIn:** Todd's primary organic channel. His headline includes a live count of clients who have gotten jobs through the program.

### Lead Nurture in DMs and Text

When Todd gets into a DM or text conversation with a potential client, he runs through 5–6 fact-finding questions to determine fit before offering a call. (Specific questions live in `voice-principles.md`.) Based on those answers, he determines whether to offer a call.

If the answer is yes, he sends a short personalized video (1–2 minutes) that recaps what they discussed and invites them to a free clarity call.

**Important:** Pricing is never raised in DM or text conversations unless the prospect explicitly asks. Before booking a clarity call, prospects fill out a routing form that includes a question confirming they can invest at the required level. Price is pre-qualified before any call happens.

### The Clarity Call

The clarity call is the primary sales conversation. By the time someone gets on a call, they have already confirmed they can pay and they have engaged with enough content or social proof to be seriously interested. The close rate once on a call is 60–70%.

The two most common reasons someone doesn't buy after a call:
1. **Timing** — after the conversation, they realize it's not the right moment.
2. **"Let me try this one thing first"** — a recruiter reached out, a connection offered to help, and they want to see if that lead converts before paying for coaching.

There is sometimes a follow-up call after the clarity call before a decision is made.

### Proof Points Used in Sales

- **Job counter:** Todd's LinkedIn headline shows a live number of clients who have gotten jobs through Launch Point. It changes consistently and is visible to anyone who follows him.
- **Video testimonials:** 40–50 video testimonials on the Launch Point website.
- **Key metrics:**
  - Average time to job offer: **3.78 months**
  - Average salary increase: **$42,000+**
  - Guarantee: **30% interview rate** — if a client completes all program requirements during the 90 days and does not average 3 interviews per 10 applications, they receive a full refund.

## Program Structure — The Interview Accelerator

### Overview

The Interview Accelerator is a 90-day group coaching program. It includes:
- A structured course (the "action plan") with 5 phases
- A quick-start onboarding module ("the Jump Start")
- Two weekly group coaching calls
- An online community (Circle.so) — called the **Ministry to Marketplace community**
- A suite of AI tools and coaching resources (Mission Control)
- An internal AI coach
- Access to Todd's personal network and the alumni network for introductions

### Onboarding

After purchase, new clients receive an initial 5-minute program walkthrough on the clarity call or a follow-up call. Group onboarding calls happen every Tuesday. First week tasks include:
- Completing the onboarding form (gives Todd context for personalized support)
- Starting the Jump Start (7-module quick-start orientation)
- Beginning Phase 1 of the action plan
- Attending the two weekly group calls immediately

### The Five-Phase Action Plan

**Phase 1: Clarity**
Getting clear on the vision for their next career and identifying specific job titles they want to pursue. Clients use the **Career Compass** tool — a proprietary assessment where Todd analyzes their input on the back end and produces a ~100-page PDF outlining 20 top job matches with detailed information. Clients then narrow that list to 2–3 target job types.

**Phase 2: Translation**
Mapping their ministry experience onto their target job types. Clients use the **Translator Coach** tool, which pulls core functions, actions, results, and required skills from live job descriptions for their target roles. Clients then identify which of those things they have done in their own history and attach objective metrics to create what we call **impact bullets** — a bank of resume-ready bullet points they will use throughout the job search.

**Phase 3: Resume**
Building a shareable master resume for each target job type, using the impact bullets from Phase 2.

**Phase 4: Team & Targets**
Identifying their personal "team" — 30–50 people in their network who will actively help their job search — and their "target organizations" — companies they are genuinely interested in and have a higher probability of getting hired at.

**Phase 5: Getting Interviews**
The active job search phase. Outreach, connection-building, conversations, and the full process of turning relationships into introductions that lead to interviews.

### Mission Control

Mission Control is the client-facing suite of AI tools and resources that supports all five phases. It includes:
- **Career Compass** — the assessment/job matching tool (Phase 1)
- **Translator Coach** — the experience translation tool (Phase 2)
- **Internal AI Coach** — currently a general Q&A chatbot; being developed into a progress-aware, just-in-time coaching tool that tracks goals, daily activity, and where each client is in the program

### The Two Weekly Group Calls

**Monday — Job Search Strategy Call (90 minutes)**
- First 30 minutes: Open Q&A
- Last 60 minutes: Identifying and solving constraints in each client's job search — tactical problem-solving to unlock better results

**Thursday — Peak Performance Call (90 minutes)**
- First 30 minutes: Open Q&A
- Last 60 minutes: Mental performance and inner-game coaching — reflection questions, group conversation, and working through limiting beliefs that are slowing clients down in their job search

## Key Differentiators

1. **Niche specificity:** Launch Point is the only career coaching program built specifically for ministry leaders making this transition. This creates a level of understanding, language, and community that no generalist coaching program can replicate.

2. **The network engine:** The program is not just a course. It is a community and alumni network that actively generates introductions — within the peer group, through alumni, and through Todd's personal professional network. The goal is not just to teach clients how to apply, but to give them relational access that leads directly to interviews.

3. **Psychological and mindset coaching:** Alongside the tactical job search skills, the program includes high-level executive coaching on the inner game — the limiting beliefs, the identity shifts, and the mental performance required to navigate a major career transition with confidence.

4. **A proven process with a guarantee:** The program follows a structured, tested system with documented results. The 30% interview rate guarantee removes the primary fear — that they will put in the work and still not get responses.

## Common Objections

**"I can't afford it" (pre-call)**
This is the most common pre-call friction. However, the routing form pre-qualifies financial readiness before any call is booked, so this rarely comes up on the call itself. Claude agents should never introduce pricing in lead nurture conversations unless directly asked.

**"The timing isn't right" (post-call)**
After talking with Todd, some clients realize they are not in the right season to fully commit. This is a legitimate objection. The response is empathetic and honest — it may genuinely not be the right time — while leaving the door open for when it is.

**"Let me try this one thing first" (post-call)**
A recruiter reached out, a connection offered help, a lead appeared. The prospect wants to see if that opportunity converts before paying for coaching. Todd's posture here is not to pressure but to stay warm and follow up when that lead goes cold — which it usually does.
